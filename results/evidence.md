# Cited evidence

Exported from the research record. Source ids resolve on OpenAlex.

- **E1** [high] Across 86 North American simulation studies, landscape fuel treatments consistently reduce wildfire extent and severity compared to untreated scenarios, but the degree of benefit is highly sensitive to treatment spatial arrangement, not just total treated area. - *Fuel treatment effectiveness at the landscape scale: a systematic review of simulation studies comparing treatment scenarios in North America* (2023) [openalex:W4321373715](https://openalex.org/W4321373715)
- **E2** [high] A 26-study empirical review found total treated area within a wildfire perimeter is highly correlated with wildfire area (r=0.89), but only 12 of 26 studies showed treatments affect fire behavior outside treatment boundaries, i.e. landscape-level spillover effects are far less established than site-level effects. - *A systematic review of empirical evidence for landscape-level fuel treatment effectiveness* (2022) [openalex:W4296231034](https://openalex.org/W4296231034)
- **E3** [medium] Optimal fuel treatment layouts that minimize total landscape fire loss form contiguous, regular spatial patterns whose shape shifts with the ignition-probability distribution and the spatial distribution of assets to protect, implying no single placement pattern (e.g. highest-load) is universally optimal across differing risk/asset layouts. - *Optimize landscape fuel treatment locations to create control opportunities for future fires* (2012) [openalex:W2055241563](https://openalex.org/W2055241563)
- **E4** [medium] A mixed-integer-programming optimizer for fuel-break placement within a landscape network, driven by large sets of simulated wildfires, produces significantly better reductions in fire size and WUI area burned than a rule-based (e.g. simple priority-ranking) placement heuristic in Southern California test cases. - *An optimization model to prioritize fuel treatments within a landscape fuel break network* (2024) [openalex:W4405494953](https://openalex.org/W4405494953)
- **E5** [medium] A fuel-break optimization framework combining burn-probability simulation, structural-loss modeling and network optimization found that even modest fuel-treatment budgets yield significant structure-loss risk reduction, and that placing breaks both inside and outside a single jurisdiction boundary outperforms single-jurisdiction placement. - *Optimizing fuel break placement to mitigate wildland fire risk to communities* (2026) [openalex:W7169504575](https://openalex.org/W7169504575)
- **E6** [high] Intensifying prescribed burning within the WUI reduces house-loss damages more than applying the same effort as landscape-wide (dispersed) treatment, but WUI-focused treatment is substantially more costly per unit area, creating a direct cost-effectiveness trade-off between asset-buffer and landscape-pattern strategies. - *Where to prescribe burn: the costs and benefits of prescribed burning close to houses* (2019) [openalex:W2948404971](https://openalex.org/W2948404971)
- **E7** [high] Across 1,617 wildfire-exposed houses in southern Australia, house destruction rates were significantly lower only where prior burning occurred within 5 years and less than 3 km upwind of the house, i.e. upwind-of-asset placement relative to the dominant wind at the time of the fire is a necessary condition for prescribed-burn effectiveness at protecting structures. - *The effectiveness of prescribed burning for protecting houses during wildfires in Australia* (2025) [openalex:W4415380118](https://openalex.org/W4415380118)
- **E8** [medium] Cost-effective prescribed-burning strategies (edge-of-asset vs dispersed landscape burning) differ by landscape and by which asset class is being protected (houses, lives, transmission lines, carbon, ecology), so a placement pattern ranked best for one landscape/asset combination need not transfer to another landscape or wind regime. - *Cost-Effective Prescribed Burning Solutions Vary Between Landscapes in Eastern Australia* (2020) [openalex:W3041214604](https://openalex.org/W3041214604)
- **E9** [medium] A complex-network / centrality-based method for siting fuel breaks (targeting network nodes that most favor fire propagation) outperformed the conventional density/flammability-based placement benchmark in both an artificial forest landscape and a real Rhodes, Greece case study. - *A Complex Network Theory Approach for the Spatial Distribution of Fire Breaks in Heterogeneous Forest Landscapes for the Control of Wildland Fires* (2016) [openalex:W2307624929](https://openalex.org/W2307624929)
- **E10** [medium] Fuel-treatment placement relative to forest cover type materially changes rate of spread, fire size and fire-type prevalence for decades after treatment, showing that *where* (not just how much) treatment is placed interacts with underlying landscape heterogeneity to determine long-term effectiveness. - *Long-Term Impacts of Fuel Treatment Placement with Respect to Forest Cover Type on Potential Fire Behavior across a Mountainous Landscape* (2019) [openalex:W2946175399](https://openalex.org/W2946175399)
- **E11** [medium] Circuit-theory fire-connectivity modeling in the Great Basin found that existing and planned fuel-break networks are denser in some high-fire-connectivity corridors than others, suggesting current fuel-break siting practice only partially aligns with where fire-spread 'centrality'/connectivity analysis would recommend breaks. - *Assessing large landscape patterns of potential fire connectivity using circuit methods* (2023) [openalex:W4362701116](https://openalex.org/W4362701116)
- **E12** [GAP] Despite widespread implementation, there is little rigorously demonstrated, generalizable evidence for linear fuel-break effectiveness, and practitioner surveys report significant controversy and negative externalities around fuel-break placement decisions; direct comparison of placement strategies (e.g. perpendicular-to-wind strips vs centrality vs asset-buffers) under a single fixed budget and varying wind direction is largely absent from the literature. - *Wildfire Response Decision Making, Risk, and Interactions with Fuels Management in California* (2026) [openalex:W7213483756](https://openalex.org/W7213483756)
- **E13** [GAP] Open gap: existing fuel-treatment optimization and spatial-pattern studies rarely stress-test a single chosen placement pattern against a change in dominant wind direction (e.g. SW vs NW) under an identical fixed-budget (~5%) scenario on the same synthetic/real landscape, leaving the robustness of 'best' placement patterns to shifting wind regimes largely untested. - *Optimize landscape fuel treatment locations to create control opportunities for future fires* (2012) [openalex:W2055241563](https://openalex.org/W2055241563)
